# AI-Native Content Design

Design stories around what current generative video does reliably enough to edit, instead of writing a conventional film and forcing the model to simulate a deterministic 3D package.

## Narrative engine before worldbuilding

A repeatable series needs:

`Hook → Conflict/Question → Escalation → Payoff → Follow/Cliffhanger`

A beautiful environment is visual material, not a narrative engine. A familiar topic is not automatically a click reason. A reusable character design is not automatically a reason to follow episode two.

## High-concept test

Prefer concepts that can be understood in one sentence and visualized quickly. A strong AI-native premise often has one visible abnormality or rule: something impossible happens, a rule is broken, a desire collides with a constraint, or a character gains/loses something with immediate consequences.

This does **not** mean FilmFoundry endorses one genre as universally profitable. Suspense, fantasy, comedy, romance, animals, history, product narrative, and other forms can work. The gate judges the actual audience, hook, payoff, series engine, production fit, and evidence.

## Production-fit design

During MVP design, reduce burdens that are fragile on the selected model surface:

- fewer simultaneous identity-critical characters;
- fewer independent actions inside one clip;
- shorter useful edit targets;
- fewer location changes;
- limited hand/prop choreography unless required;
- voice-over rather than forced lip sync when speech synchronization is not the product;
- hard cuts between designed compositions instead of blind tail-frame chaining;
- keyframes that already contain the expensive visual truth.

If the provider profile later proves stronger capability, spend that capability where it creates viewer value rather than adding complexity for its own sake.

## Thirty-episode engine

“Thirty topics exist” is not enough. A series engine explains why the same audience wants another episode. Examples of engines include escalating rules, recurring character goals, progressive mystery, repeatable transformation, competition, collection, relationship progression, or a stable format with genuinely new consequences.

Write the engine before creating thirty assets.
